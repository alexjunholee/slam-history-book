# Chapter 7. Certifiable Global Optimality

The trajectory from Lu and Milios to g2o and GTSAM gave SLAM fast, scalable backends, yet left one uncomfortable flaw intact: pose graph optimization is non-convex, so Gauss-Newton and Levenberg-Marquardt can silently settle into local minima. For years, practitioners leaned on a comfortable rule of thumb—*"start from a decent odometry guess, and it usually solves fine"*—even though deployed backends occasionally twisted into completely wrong maps without issuing any warning. Beginning in 2015, Luca Carlone at MIT set out to replace that empirical faith with mathematical guarantees. His work on Lagrangian duality catalyzed David Rosen's SE-Sync (2019), Briales and Gonzalez-Jimenez's Cartan-Sync, Yang and Carlone's TEASER, and Papalia's CORA—transforming SLAM backends from local optimizers that *usually* work into convex relaxations capable of issuing certificates of global optimality. Remarkably, every tool powering that transformation was borrowed from outside SLAM: Shor relaxation from operations research, Burer-Monteiro factorization from mathematical optimization, Riemannian optimization from differential geometry, and Kirchhoff's Matrix-Tree Theorem from nineteenth-century graph theory.

---

## 7.1 The Old Anxiety of Local Minima

As Chapter 6 §6.7 noted, sensitivity to initialization is the Achilles' heel of any graph SLAM backend. Because the objective is non-convex in the 3D rotation matrices $\boldsymbol{R}_i \in \mathrm{SO}(3)$, Gauss-Newton can slide into the wrong basin whenever the initial pose guess strays too far from the truth. The multi-story parking-garage example in Handbook §6.1 illustrates this starkly: out of four random initializations, only one lands at the same global minimum as SE-Sync, while the other three twist into absurd local minima with the garage floors visibly folded into one another.

Through the late 2000s, robotics engineers defended against that hazard in two practical ways: feeding the solver as clean an odometry initialization as possible, and aggressively pruning false loop closures at the front end. Both habits helped, yet neither could tell whether a converged solution was truly the global optimum. Around 2010, Shoudong Huang and Gamini Dissanayake highlighted a deeper snag: even starting from a good initial guess, ambiguous measurements can still stall the optimizer at the wrong solution. Around the same time, pose graph optimization (PGO) was formally proven NP-hard in the worst case—even though tools like g2o kept succeeding routinely on everyday robot logs.

By the mid-2010s, backend theorists zeroed in on that striking gap between worst-case complexity and real-world success. In optimization, convergence of Gauss-Newton guarantees at most local stationarity: under ideal second-order conditions, a local minimum exhibits zero gradient and a positive-semidefinite Hessian, and finite-precision numerical solvers may even halt early due to tolerances or poor conditioning. Worst of all, failure is hardest to spot precisely when a backend calmly reports convergence.

> 🔗 **Borrowed.** Chapter 6's robust kernels (Huber, Cauchy) and graduated non-convexity (GNC) share a common root in the robust statistics and duality theorem of [Black & Rangarajan (1996)](https://cs.brown.edu/people/mjblack/Papers/ijcv1996.pdf). One branch adjusted cost weights to damp out outliers; the other harnessed that same duality to steer around non-convexity.

## 7.2 Shor Relaxation — A Tool From Operations Research

Every bit of non-convexity in PGO traces back to the rotation constraint $\boldsymbol{R}_i \in \mathrm{SO}(d)$. Orthogonality ($\boldsymbol{R}^\top \boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{I}$) is already quadratic, and although $\det(\boldsymbol{R})=+1$ looks cubic in three dimensions, right-handedness can be rewritten equivalently using quadratic cross-product relations among the matrix columns. Paired with a quadratic cost function, those constraints turn PGO into a standard **QCQP** (Quadratically Constrained Quadratic Program)—a class of problems for which operations research had developed a powerful convex relaxation back in 1987: [Naum Shor's relaxation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01582220).

Shor's trick rests on the trace identity $\boldsymbol{x}^\top \boldsymbol{M}\boldsymbol{x} = \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{M}\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}^\top)$. By introducing a lifted symmetric matrix variable $\boldsymbol{X} \triangleq \boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}^\top$, every quadratic term in the original QCQP becomes purely *linear* in $\boldsymbol{X}$, concentrating all non-convexity into two requirements: $\boldsymbol{X} \succeq 0$ and rank 1. Dropping that stubborn rank-1 constraint yields a pure convex **semidefinite program (SDP)**—expanding the search space from $n$ variables to $n(n+1)/2$, yet making global optimization tractable in polynomial time:

$$d^* = \min_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathbb{S}^n} \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{X}) \;\; \text{s.t.} \;\; \mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{A}_i\boldsymbol{X})=b_i,\; \boldsymbol{X}\succeq 0.$$

The magic of this relaxation flows from weak duality, $d^* \le p^*$: the SDP optimum $d^*$ can never exceed the true global minimum $p^*$ of the original QCQP. Consequently, for any candidate solution $\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}$, comparing its objective value against $d^*$ via $f(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}) - d^*$ immediately upper-bounds how far $\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}$ can possibly sit from global optimality—giving birth to the word "certifiable." Better still, whenever the SDP solution $\boldsymbol{X}^*$ happens to come out rank 1, it factors cleanly as $\boldsymbol{X}^* = \boldsymbol{x}^*\boldsymbol{x}^{*\top}$, certifying $\boldsymbol{x}^*$ as a true global minimizer of the original non-convex problem. The natural follow-up question was how often SLAM graphs enjoy that exact zero-gap miracle.

That question launched a wave of work with two 2015 papers by Luca Carlone at IROS and ICRA ([Carlone et al. 2015 "Lagrangian duality in 3D SLAM"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.00746) and [Carlone & Dellaert 2015 "Planar pose graph optimization"](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICRA.2015.7139264)). Having just completed his 2014 TRO survey of initialization methods for g2o and GTSAM—where he saw firsthand how often conflicting odometry and loop closures trapped local solvers—Carlone tested Lagrangian duality on real benchmarks and discovered empirically that the duality gap vanished in nearly every 2D PGO instance, while sketching the extension to 3D. Even though those 2015 papers could not yet specify exact conditions guaranteeing a zero gap, [Briales & Gonzalez-Jimenez (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03235) soon extended the framework to SO(3) synchronization with Cartan-Sync. Meanwhile, Nicolas Boumal, P.-A. Absil, and Rodolphe Sepulchre were advancing Riemannian optimization just as Burer and Monteiro's low-rank SDP factorization from 2003 waited in the wings—setting the stage for David Rosen and his collaborators to bring all the pieces together in 2019.

## 7.3 SE-Sync — What Rosen 2019 Assembled

Published in *IJRR* in 2019, [Rosen, Carlone, Bandeira, and Leonard's SE-Sync](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07386) became the landmark reference for certifiable SLAM. Rosen had earned his PhD at MIT under John Leonard—the same Leonard who, alongside Durrant-Whyte in Chapter 4, helped christen the name "SLAM" in the early 1990s—and teamed up with Carlone, Leonard, and applied mathematician Afonso Bandeira, who supplied the theoretical proof guaranteeing the global optimality of rank-deficient second-order critical points. Uniting robotics, SLAM, mathematical optimization, and applied mathematics, SE-Sync fused Shor relaxation, analytical translation elimination, Burer-Monteiro low-rank parameterization, and Boumal's Riemannian Staircase into a single PGO solver.

SE-Sync executes in three clean steps. First, noticing that translations reduce to linear least squares once rotations are fixed—a property Carlone had highlighted in his 2014 TRO survey—the algorithm eliminates $\boldsymbol{t}$ analytically in closed form (Problem 6.2). Second, Shor relaxation lifts the remaining rotation-only problem $\min_{\boldsymbol{R}\in\mathrm{SO}(d)^n} \mathrm{tr}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}\boldsymbol{R}^\top\boldsymbol{R})$ into a convex SDP (Problem 6.3). Third, because standard interior-point SDP solvers choke once a $dn \times dn$ matrix reaches a few thousand poses, Burer-Monteiro reparameterization ($\boldsymbol{Z} = \boldsymbol{Y}^\top \boldsymbol{Y}$) recasts the SDP into a compact, unconstrained optimization problem over the smooth Stiefel manifold (Problem 6.4).

Two complementary theorems underwrite the algorithm's reliability. Theorem 6.1 establishes **exact recovery**: whenever measurement noise stays below a threshold $\beta$, the SDP relaxation admits a unique solution $\boldsymbol{Z}^*$ of rank $d$ that recovers the exact global minimum of the original maximum-likelihood problem. (Unlike a vector QCQP whose exact solution has rank 1, SE-Sync stacks rotations as $\boldsymbol{Z}=\boldsymbol{R}^\top\boldsymbol{R}$ with $\boldsymbol{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times dn}$, so an exact recovery has rank $d$.) The catch is that $\beta$ depends on the unknown ground-truth matrix and cannot be evaluated beforehand. Theorem 6.2—adapted from Boumal et al.—closes that practical gap by proving that any rank-deficient second-order critical point on the Stiefel manifold is a global minimum of the SDP. Together, the two theorems power the **Riemannian Staircase**: start at low rank, locate a second-order critical point, test for rank deficiency, and increment the rank by one if needed. Because every $\boldsymbol{Y}$ is guaranteed to be row-rank-deficient once rank reaches $dn + 1$, the staircase terminates in finitely many steps—and on typical SLAM benchmarks, a single step almost always suffices.

Across standard benchmarks such as `sphere`, `torus`, and `garage`, SE-Sync matched the speed of g2o and GTSAM while delivering something neither local solver could: an a posteriori certificate. Where g2o and GTSAM simply return an answer and fall silent, Rosen's solver outputs an explicit suboptimality bound—and whenever that bound hits zero, the map is provably globally optimal. (Conversely, a nonzero gap warns that optimality is uncertified, without proving the candidate suboptimal.) More than twenty years after Lu and Milios, a SLAM backend could finally prove its own solution right.

> 📜 **Prediction vs. outcome.** In §8.2 of the IJRR 2019 paper, Rosen wrote that *"the algebraic simplification we have shown could be extended to anisotropic noise, outliers, and a variety of sensor modalities."* That forecast proved largely prescient: Holmes and Barfoot's 2023 landmark-SLAM extension, Papalia's 2024 CORA for range measurements, and Yang and Carlone's TEASER line soon followed. Yet the most coveted prize—bringing SE-Sync-style certification to perspective projection in visual SLAM—remained unrealized as of 2026, because rational camera projection resists folding cleanly into polynomial optimization.

> 🔗 **Borrowed.** The Burer-Monteiro factorization at the heart of SE-Sync is the low-rank SDP method of [Burer & Monteiro (2003)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-002-0352-8), later sharpened by [Boumal-Voroninski-Bandeira (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08101)'s Riemannian proof of the globality of second-order critical points, which Rosen brought into SLAM. From pure math to the robot backend, sixteen years.

## 7.4 The Unexpected Equivalence of Graph Laplacian and Fisher Information

Once a backend certifies that it has found the true global minimum, a natural second question arises: *how close is that global minimum to physical reality?* Classical estimation theory answers that question through the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) and the **Fisher Information Matrix (FIM)**. In a simplified PGO model with fixed rotations, Rosen, Khosoussi, and Barfoot uncovered a striking identity: the FIM equals nothing more than the Kronecker product of the graph's weighted reduced Laplacian with the identity matrix:

$$\mathcal{I} = \boldsymbol{J}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} \boldsymbol{J} = \boldsymbol{L}_w \otimes \boldsymbol{I}_3.$$

This compact equation carries remarkable power: purely from the *connectivity graph*—even before collecting a single physical sensor reading—one can quantify how accurate the final pose estimate will be. By Kirchhoff's Matrix-Tree Theorem, the determinant of the reduced Laplacian counts the graph's weighted spanning trees, linking directly to D-optimality (maximizing the determinant of the information matrix), while the graph's algebraic connectivity (Fiedler value) governs E-optimality (bounding worst-case estimation variance). By the 2020s, Kirchhoff's 1847 theorem for electrical resistor networks had become the mathematical foundation of measurement selection and active SLAM—turning "maximize information" into spectral optimization of the graph Laplacian.

[The post-2014 work of Kasra Khosoussi and Timothy Barfoot](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08601) (building from Khosoussi's Sydney doctorate under Dissanayake and Huang before continuing at MIT and Toronto) extended this bridge to full 3D PGO, where the Laplacian combines via Kronecker product with the SE(3) adjoint representation so topological structure and geometric weighting decouple cleanly. Because the reduced Laplacian is six times smaller than the full FIM, its spectrum offers a fast, rigorous mathematical criterion for the "loop-closure selection" problem left open in Chapter 6.

Equally illuminating, the CRLB lens casts fresh light on the EKF-SLAM consistency breakdown seen in Chapter 4's "Still open" section: the overconfidence Julier and Uhlmann exposed in 2001 amounts to linearization falsely inflating the Fisher information beyond its true physical limit. That is precisely why Handbook §6.2 pairs Fisher information analysis directly beside §6.1's convex relaxation—certifying a global minimum and measuring how trustworthy it is form two halves of the same theory.

> 🔗 **Borrowed.** [Kirchhoff's Matrix-Tree Theorem (1847)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirchhoff%27s_theorem), born as an analysis tool for electrical circuits, was transplanted through combinatorics into measurement-design literature and, in the 2010s through Khosoussi, became the language of SLAM active perception—completing a 180-year migration path.

## 7.5 Extensions and Limits — TEASER, CORA, and Lasserre's Wall

With SE-Sync having cracked clean pose graphs, certifiable optimization pushed outward along two fronts: resisting severe **outliers** and expanding beyond relative poses to handle range measurements, landmarks, and anisotropic noise.

Outliers demanded attention first. As Chapter 6 §6.7 noted, front-end loop-closure detectors inevitably let false matches slip through—and beyond a modest contamination ratio, even Huber or Cauchy robust losses collapse. Around 2017, researchers began crafting certifiably outlier-robust solvers, led prominently by [Yang, Shi, and Carlone's TEASER (TRO 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07715). On 3D point-cloud registration benchmarks, TEASER recovered globally optimal alignments even with up to 99% outlier correspondences by solving truncated least squares inside a graduated non-convexity (GNC) wrapper, decoupling scale, translation, and rotation into separate certifiable subproblems with SDP relaxation on rotation. Soon after, [Yang & Carlone (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03349) generalized this machinery via Lasserre moment relaxations under the banner of "certifiably robust estimation."

Sensor-modality extensions advanced alongside outlier robustness. For range-aided SLAM, [Papalia et al.'s CORA (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11614) tackled distance residuals $(\|\boldsymbol{t}_j - \boldsymbol{t}_i\| - \tilde r_{ij})^2$, whose implicit square root prevents them from forming a direct QCQP. By introducing an auxiliary unit-bearing vector $\boldsymbol{b}_{ij} \in S^{d-1}$ ("bearing lifting"), CORA reformulated range-aided SLAM into a clean QCQP, demonstrating tight relaxations in single-robot settings while showing that tightness can break down in multi-robot configurations—sharpening the boundary of where Shor relaxation holds. On the landmark front, [Holmes & Barfoot (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05631) applied the Schur complement to eliminate landmarks beforehand, yielding a reduced PGO directly solvable by SE-Sync; in 2025, Holmes, Khosoussi, and Rosen co-authored Chapter 6 of *The SLAM Handbook*.

Yet stubborn mathematical walls also emerged. Folding anisotropic noise and truncated-quadratic robust losses into a general Polynomial Optimization Problem (POP) requires Lasserre's moment relaxation, whose lifted SDP matrices frequently turn **degenerate**, breaking the constraint qualification conditions required for the Riemannian Staircase's convergence guarantee. (Degeneracy voids the guarantee without necessarily implying divergence on every instance.) Yang's 2022 sparse monomial basis partially sidesteps that bottleneck, though its specialized solver remains markedly slower than local Gauss-Newton. Meanwhile, Visual SLAM and VIO pose deeper algebraic hurdles around perspective projection and IMU preintegration, explored in the 🧭 section below.

> 📜 **Prediction vs. outcome.** At ICRA 2015, Carlone observed that a *"theoretical explanation for why most instances have a tight Lagrangian dual is needed."* A decade later, that answer remains half-finished: Rosen, Carlone, Bandeira, and Leonard proved exact recovery whenever noise sits below $\beta$, yet offered no way to evaluate $\beta$ ahead of time on a live problem. As of 2026, no **a priori** test predicts when tightness breaks; per-instance a posteriori certificates still carry the load.

## Still open

**The boundary where tightness breaks.** Although SE-Sync's exact-recovery theorem guarantees tightness for noise below $\beta$, computing $\beta$ on an actual problem instance remains impossible without knowing ground truth. An a priori diagnostic for tightness—along with a systematic characterization of how relaxations fail under severe outliers or ultra-sparse topologies—would directly guide real-world algorithm design.

**Integration with visual SLAM and VIO.** Pinhole perspective projection $\pi(\boldsymbol{X}) = [X/Z, Y/Z]$ is a rational function rather than a polynomial. Clearing the denominator introduces a fresh auxiliary variable and constraint for every landmark observation, quickly blowing up an SDP beyond real-time feasibility across the thousands of map points maintained by systems like ORB-SLAM3. Likewise, Forster's 2015 IMU preintegration intertwines Lie-group exponential maps with bias drift that resist clean POP reformulation. As of 2026, the visual and VIO mainstream covered in Chapter 8, Chapter 11, and Chapter 16 remains largely outside certifiable guarantees—the lineage's single biggest gap.

**Online certification and scale.** SE-Sync is fundamentally a batch solver. Just as Square Root SAM evolved into iSAM2 for incremental SLAM, certifiable estimation needs incremental solvers capable of updating certificates as each new measurement arrives. Warm starts, incremental rank elevation, and composing partial certificates remain active research problems, while general moment-relaxation solvers remain far too slow at city scale.

**Outlier-majority.** While estimators like TEASER achieve striking robustness even with 99% outliers in registration, those guarantees do not yet carry over universally to arbitrary SLAM graphs and sensor contamination patterns. When extreme ambiguity leaves multiple competing interpretations plausible, multi-hypothesis frameworks such as list-decodable regression—explored by Cheng, Shi, and Carlone around 2024—offer an intriguing direction, though none has yet matured into a standard tool alongside TEASER.
